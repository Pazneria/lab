# Room study: references and translation

The room is an original pale-stone and sage research workshop with three
different volumes. A lower entrance gallery opens onto a 6.4 m benchmark hall
with a roof lantern and high west glazing. A 4.6 m apparatus bay has oak beams,
a projecting glass window and a dark soapstone work surface. Across the hall,
a 2.9 m oak study alcove has books, a writing desk and a recording-instrument
study. Piers and material thresholds frame views between these areas; walking
stays on one level floor. A brass-edged runner leads to the useful computer.
The research tools stay native 2D pages.

Three primary institutional references were read, with their actual source
images inspected on 2026-09-30. This is design interpretation, not a replica or
an affiliation claim. No reference photograph, logo, or historical instrument
model was copied into the website.

| Reference | Verified source | Visual lesson used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Faraday's magnetic laboratory | [Royal Institution — Faraday Museum](https://www.rigb.org/visit/faraday-museum). The institution describes the laboratory as displayed in its 1850s state. The banner photograph by Paul Wilkinson shows work benches, exposed apparatus, shelving, and light wall surfaces. | A room that feels occupied by actual work: shallow shelves, visible apparatus, and a central bench. The original timber finish is translated to sage cabinets and pale mineral surfaces, distinct from Arcade. |
| Salk's architecture | [Salk — virtual architecture tour](https://www.salk.edu/about/visiting-salk/virtual-architecture-tour/). The institutional tour describes adaptable open laboratory floors and abundant daylight; its hero photograph shows the pale courtyard and clear central axis. | Large windows, a simple material palette, open circulation, and a clear line of sight to the useful station. No campus, water feature, or Kahn building replica. |
| Bell Labs instrument work | [Nokia Bell Labs — history](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/history/). The institutional page contains a historical recording-turntable photograph showing close instrument work under a task light. | A quiet brass task lamp and compact instrument details. This image is the reference, rather than an unsupported claim about a particular Bell Labs building layout. |

The prototype has one catalog computer and a home door. Other apparatus is
decorative and does not promise a project or simulation. It contains no
VoterIntelligence station, fabricated metrics, placeholder graphs, countdowns,
coming-soon messaging, slogans, or speculative second rooms.

Opus's architectural pass gives the workshop variation through ceiling height,
window depth, piers, a low study portal, contrasting work surfaces and a quieter
desk. Apparatus is composed into recognizable groups: an electromagnet, bell
jar and pump, retort stand, wound coil and balance. These are original geometric
studies, not simulations or claims about historical instrument reconstruction.
The Bell Labs reference informs the recording-instrument desk under a task
lamp; the Salk reference informs daylight and a clear central axis; Faraday's
laboratory informs exposed apparatus and visible work. No reference image is
shipped as a texture. The room is still one bounded first room.

The room should be judged as a small first direction: welcoming daylight,
visible work, a recognizable destination, and calm controls. The flat plan
retains the same spatial landmarks without forcing motion or WebGL. Phones
get a direction pad over the room; native actions remain outside the canvas.
